# Stakeholder Interview

**Due** Wed Sep 9, statement and confirmation · **Explain-back** Thu Sep 10 or
Fri Sep 11, booked · **10 points** · Weeks 1 and 2

## What this is for

You cannot build the right thing until someone who has the problem agrees you
understand it. This is the assessment that checks you do, and it runs first
because everything after it inherits the answer.

**What is graded is your stakeholder's confirmation, not your summary of the
meeting.** A summary is exactly what AI produces well from a transcript, so
grading one would measure transcription. Getting a busy person to read your
description of their problem and say "yes, that's it" is a different skill, and
it is the one that matters.

## What you do

**1. Research before you meet.** Learn what you can about the stakeholder's
organization, their role, and the domain before you take an hour of their time.
You are looking for the questions you would not have thought to ask cold.

**2. Hold the meeting.** Record it and have AI summarize it. That is a good use
of AI and you are expected to do it. Your attention during the meeting is better
spent listening than writing.

**3. Write a short problem statement with success criteria.** One page is
plenty. It says who has the problem, what the problem is, what makes it worth
solving, and how you would know it was solved. The success criteria are the
seed of the KPIs and thresholds your PRD will carry, so write them as things
that could be measured rather than as adjectives.

**4. Get it confirmed in writing.** Send the statement to your stakeholder and
ask them to reply confirming it is accurate, or telling you what is wrong. If
they tell you what is wrong, fix it and send it again. A corrected statement
that ends in a genuine confirmation is a better outcome than one that was right
first time, and it is worth the same marks.

**5. Explain it back.** In a 10-minute meeting in week 2, tell me your
stakeholder's problem without notes. See below.

## What to submit

In `stakeholder-interview/` in your project repository, by Wed Sep 9:

`problem-statement.md` is the statement itself, in its confirmed form.

`confirmation.md` holds your stakeholder's written confirmation. Paste the email
or message, with the sender and the date visible. If they confirmed a corrected
version, include the exchange that got there rather than only the final yes.

`research.md` records what you learned before the meeting and, specifically,
**which question you asked because of it.** One question, named. This is the
part students skip, and it is the difference between research and reading.

## The explain-back

Ten minutes, booked at https://calendly.com/greg-lontok/30min for Thu Sep 10 or
Fri Sep 11. Bring nothing.

I will ask you to tell me your stakeholder's problem, who has it, and how you
would know it was solved. Then I will ask follow-up questions. There is nothing
to prepare beyond having done the work, and no way to prepare for it other than
understanding your own project.

This exists because everything else in this assessment is a document, and a
document can be produced by someone who does not understand it. Ten minutes of
conversation cannot. It is the same check the PRD defense makes at the Sprint 01
review, and the same one the capstone showcase makes at the end. You will do
this a lot in this course, and this is the low-stakes version.

### What specific means

Generic: *"The organization struggles with inefficient manual processes and needs
a solution to improve productivity."* That describes almost every organization,
which means it describes none.

Specific: *"Three staff spend the first two hours of every Monday copying the
weekend's orders out of email into the fulfillment spreadsheet, and errors from
that step surface as wrong shipments about twice a month."*

The second one names who, how often, how long, and what it costs. It can be
wrong, which is what makes it worth confirming. Aim for something your
stakeholder could disagree with.

### What the explain-back is looking for

Not fluency. Whether the problem is yours yet.

Strong: you describe the problem in your stakeholder's terms, you know why it
matters to them specifically, you can say what you do not yet know, and a
follow-up question gets a real answer rather than a restatement.

Weak: you reproduce your problem statement out loud, and a follow-up question
that steps outside it has nowhere to go.

## If your stakeholder goes quiet

Tell me before Wed Sep 9 rather than after. Stakeholders disappear, and it is a
normal thing that happens in real projects, not a failure on your part. The
re-baseline protocol in the syllabus covers this and we will find you a path.
What does not work is silence until the deadline has passed.
